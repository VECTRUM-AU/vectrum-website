Workflow

How it works.

Four steps from spreadsheet to Google Earth in under ten seconds.

01
Prepare Excel

Your spreadsheet needs Site ID, Lat, Lon, RAT (2G/3G/4G/5G), Azimuth, and optionally tower height. Download our template to get started instantly.

02
Drop & Map

Drop your file into SectorDrop. Map your column names to the correct fields. Choose RAT colours and beamwidth.

03
Generate

SectorDrop calculates triangular sector polygons with correct azimuth bearings, stacked RAT lengths, and 3D height placement — entirely in your browser.

04
Export to GE

Download the KMZ. Open in Google Earth Pro. Your sectors appear with coloured RAT layers, correct folders, and 3D tilt support.

Capabilities

Built for RF engineers.

Everything a field engineer, optimizer, or contractor actually needs — nothing they don't.

📶
Multi-RAT Stacking

2G sectors are longest, 3G shorter, 4G shorter still, 5G shortest. Correct visual hierarchy so you can distinguish technology layers at a glance.

🧭
Correct Azimuth Geometry

Sectors point in the exact bearing you specify. Triangular polygon geometry built from the azimuth midpoint with configurable beamwidth.

🏗️
3D Height Support

Define tower height per site or use a global default. Sectors render at the correct AGL elevation in Google Earth's 3D view.

🎨
Custom RAT Colours

Pick any hex colour for each RAT. KMZ layers are named and coloured consistently so Google Earth folders match your colour scheme.

🔒
100% Browser-Local

Your site coordinates never leave your machine. No uploads, no servers, no logs. Suitable for operator data confidentiality requirements.

📁
Clean KMZ Structure

Output includes separate Google Earth folders per RAT, descriptive placemarks with site info, and optimised polygon count for smooth rendering.

Excel template.

Your spreadsheet needs these columns. Column names are flexible — you map them in the tool. Download the template to see the exact format.

Column Example Notes

Each row = one sector. A 3-sector site has 3 rows with the same SiteID, Lat, Lon but different RAT/Azimuth values.
